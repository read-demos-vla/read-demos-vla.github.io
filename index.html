READ: What Makes a Pretrained VLA Teachable by Demonstration?

AuthorsAnonymousStatusUnder double-blind reviewPolicyπ0.5, pretrained weights frozen

Show a pretrained robot policy five demonstrations of a new task, and it can perform the task. No gradient step, no fine-tuning run: the demonstrations are written once into a memory that the policy reads while it acts.

One scene, one instruction, move the bread. Only the five demonstrations change, and the robot follows them. Paper checkpoints and demonstrations, re-recorded at 4× resolution on the first evaluation seed (not selected), 2× speed. The bread was never manipulated in training.

01

Write the demonstrations once, read them at every step

READ adds a demonstration encoder and a few gated readers to π0.5. The encoder turns five demonstrations (camera video, robot states and actions) into a fixed memory. While the policy generates each action chunk, readers in its action expert query that memory, so different moments can draw on different parts of the demonstrations.

π0.5 acting, with readers in its action expert
in-context training

The stage between pretraining and adaptation. The encoder, the readers and one LoRA shared by all tasks are trained once across many training tasks; π0.5's pretrained weights stay frozen. A new task then costs one encoder pass. This is the stage we vary to ask which training experience makes a policy teachable.

What in the training data makes this possible?

Two READ models see nearly the same number of demonstrations: each of 37 objects in five directions. The shared model trains every object in the same five; the scattered model varies them across objects, covering nine directions overall. Neither has trained this mouse at 240° or 300°, but only the scattered model has seen other objects move that way.

More kinds of behavior in training, more that demonstrations can teach

06

Where it stops

Shown circling, dropping or shaking, the broad-set model picks the object up and falls back to behaviors it was trained on. At nearly fixed data, coverage decided what transferred in both suites; these failures mark what training should cover next.

Each pair: one of the five demonstrations (another episode of the task, so the scene differs), then READ on its own test episode, 2× speed. Narrow set 0/900 and broad set 17/900 on the six held-out behaviors, mostly tapping.

07

Five demonstrations match per-task fine-tuning

On ten held-out LIBERO-Gen tasks, which pair familiar pick-up places and destinations in new ways, READ reaches 92.8% from five demonstrations; a LoRA fine-tuned on each task with all 36–49 of its demonstrations reaches 92.5%. The demonstrations matter most where the instruction is hard to ground:

08

Questions

Isn't this just fine-tuning on the new task?

No parameter changes on the new task. Five demonstrations are encoded once into a cached memory, and the policy acts. For comparison, the per-task LoRA that READ matches on LIBERO-Gen takes 24k gradient steps on 36–49 demonstrations; fine-tuned on the same five demonstrations READ receives, it reaches 82.2%.

The instruction already names the task. Are the demonstrations even used?

In the Direction and Behavior suites the instruction names only the object, so the goal or behavior can come only from the demonstrations: base π0.5 succeeds in 0 of 630 Direction episodes. Swapping demonstrations redirects the object (section 03), and on LIBERO-Gen swapping them lowers READ's success more than the weight-generating baseline's.

Why a memory, and not weights generated from the demonstrations?

Our Demo2LoRA baseline uses the same encoder, data and loss but writes the demonstrations into per-task LoRA weights before execution. READ keeps a memory the policy can query as its situation changes: 92.8% vs 87.2% on LIBERO-Gen with full instructions, and stronger dependence on demonstration content.

What does it cost at deployment?

One encoder pass per task, cached as 0.2 MiB of memory tokens. Each action chunk takes 5.7% longer than the base policy on one L40S (LIBERO-Gen, 100-token memory); the readers query a few hundred cached tokens and video is never re-encoded during rollouts.

Can it learn completely new behaviors from demonstrations?

Not yet at our data scale. It transfers trained movements to new objects and new pairings, and trained behaviors to nearby distances and angles, but six held-out behaviors stay near zero (section 06). Broader coverage was what helped in both suites, which is why we read these results as a guide for scaling training data.

Are these results in the real world?

No. All results are in simulation, on LIBERO-Gen and on two RoboTwin benchmarks we built so that the training tasks could be rearranged.
